# Vimy research: the leader-rating rubric

Every leader in `scenarios/vimy-1917/leaders.yaml` may carry five ratings, each an integer from 1 to 5
(`packages/scenario/src/schema.ts`, `LeaderSchema`). This note says what a 1, a 3 and a 5 look like, in terms of
conduct a source can record, so that a rating can be argued from the record and checked by someone else. A 2 or a 4
sits between the descriptions on either side of it.

**The ratings are the owner's.** The builder drafted the corps, division and brigade commanders' ratings as
`estimate`s, each with a justification citing conduct (`leaders.md`); the owner confirms or changes them. A 3 whose
justification says no conduct was found is a placeholder midpoint, not a judgement that the commander was average.

**What counts as evidence.** Conduct in the Vimy operation (planning from January 1917 to 14 April 1917) first; conduct
in the nearest earlier or later battles only where Vimy gives nothing, and the justification says so. A result alone
(an objective taken on time) is weak evidence of the commander's part in it, so it supports a 4 at most unless the
source ties the result to the commander's own decision.

## planning

How well the commander's preparation fitted the task: reconnaissance, rehearsal, allotment of troops to objectives,
foresight of obstacles.

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Objectives, timings or routes not worked out; units arrive at the start line without knowing their task; a known obstacle (wire, a strongpoint, a flank) is not provided for, and the attack breaks on it. |
| 3 | A sound plan on the standard pattern of the day: objectives and timings set, units briefed, routine provision for mopping up and consolidation; nothing anticipated beyond the routine. |
| 5 | Preparation beyond the routine that the record ties to the outcome: rehearsals on a model of the ground, a specific obstacle foreseen and provided for (extra wire-cutters for uncut wire, a flank guard for an exposed flank), every level briefed on its task. |

## orderSpeed

How quickly the commander turned news into orders that reached the troops.

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hours pass between a report and an order; orders arrive after the moment they were meant for, or not at all. |
| 3 | Orders follow events at the usual pace of the day's communications (runners, telephone where it survived): reserves committed within an hour or two of the need being known. |
| 5 | Orders issued within minutes of the news, on the commander's own initiative, and acted on in time: a reserve sent before it is asked for, a flank formed as soon as a gap is reported. |

## adaptiveness

Whether the commander changed the plan when the situation departed from it, and whether the change fitted the new
situation.

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Holds to the plan after it has visibly failed: repeats a failed attack on the same lines, or leaves a reserve unused while a neighbour's flank hangs open. |
| 3 | Follows the plan while it works and makes the obvious adjustments when it does not (a defensive flank, a delayed bound), usually when ordered from above. |
| 5 | Re-tasks troops to meet the unforeseen on their own reading of the situation, and the change works: a reserve switched to the critical point, a timetable recast to let tired troops recover, an exploitation seized. |

## riskTolerance

How much the commander was willing to stake for a gain: high is neither good nor bad in itself.

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Will not move without full artillery preparation and secure flanks; stops on the first objective rather than push into the unknown. |
| 3 | Accepts the risks inherent in the set-piece plan of the day, and no more. |
| 5 | Attacks without the planned support or with open flanks when the chance offers, or pushes beyond the set objectives on their own authority. |

## moraleEffect

The commander's effect on the troops' willingness to go on, as the record shows it.

| Rating | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | The record shows the troops' confidence in the commander lost: complaints, refusals, the commander blamed by those under them. |
| 3 | No particular effect recorded either way. |
| 5 | The record shows the troops' confidence attributed to the commander: visible leadership in the line, the men's trust cited by contemporaries. |
